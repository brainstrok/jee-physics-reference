## 1. Units and Measurements

### Core ideas
- Physics is quantitative; every physical quantity requires a unit.
- SI base units: meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), candela (cd).
- Derived units are combinations of base units.
- Dimensions express the nature of a physical quantity.

### Important formulas
- Density: ρ = m / V
- Speed: v = distance / time
- Force: F = ma
- Pressure: P = F / A
- Work: W = F·s
- Power: P = W / t

### Dimensional formulas
- Area = [L²]
- Volume = [L³]
- Velocity = [LT⁻¹]
- Acceleration = [LT⁻²]
- Force = [MLT⁻²]
- Work / Energy = [ML²T⁻²]
- Power = [ML²T⁻³]
- Pressure = [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

### Errors
- Absolute error: Δa = |a_measured − a_true|
- Relative error: δa = Δa / a
- Percentage error: % error = (Δa / a) × 100
- Significant figures: rules for zeros, trailing zeros, and decimal numbers

### Tricks
- Always check dimensions before accepting a formula.
- Convert to SI first.
- Use limiting-case reasoning for MCQ checking.

## 2. Vectors

### Key idea
- Scalar: only magnitude
- Vector: magnitude + direction

### Formulas
- Dot product: a·b = ab cos θ
- Cross product: |a×b| = ab sin θ
- Resultant of two vectors: R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cos θ)
- Direction: tan α = (B sin θ)/(A + B cos θ)
- Unit vector: â = a / |a|
- Components: a = a_x î + a_y ĵ + a_z k̂
- Magnitude: |a| = √(a_x² + a_y² + a_z²)

### Applications
- Work: W = F·s
- Torque: τ = r × F
- Area of parallelogram = |a × b|
- Area of triangle = 1/2 |a × b|

### Common mistakes
- Not resolving vectors into components
- Using wrong sign in direction
- Confusing displacement and distance

## 3. Kinematics

### 1D equations
- v = u + at
- s = ut + 1/2 at²
- v² = u² + 2as
- Average velocity = displacement / time
- Average acceleration = Δv / Δt

### Motion under gravity
- v = u − gt
- s = ut − 1/2 gt²
- v² = u² − 2gh

### Projectile motion
- Horizontal component: u_x = u cos θ
- Vertical component: u_y = u sin θ
- Time of flight: T = 2u sin θ / g
- Maximum height: H = u² sin²θ / 2g
- Horizontal range: R = u² sin 2θ / g
- Equation of trajectory: y = x tan θ − (g x²)/(2u² cos²θ)

### Relative motion
- v_AB = v_A − v_B
- a_AB = a_A − a_B

### Graphs
- x−t graph: slope = velocity
- v−t graph: slope = acceleration
- area under v−t = displacement

## 4. Laws of Motion

### Newton's laws
- First law: inertia
- Second law: F = ma
- Third law: action reaction pairs act on different bodies

### Important formulas
- Weight: W = mg
- Friction: f ≤ μN
- Static friction: f_s ≤ μ_s N
- Kinetic friction: f_k = μ_k N
- Spring force: F = −kx

### Circular motion basics
- Angular speed: ω = v / r
- Centripetal acceleration: a_c = v² / r = rω²
- Centripetal force: F_c = mv² / r = mrω²

### Banking of roads
- tan θ = v² / (rg)

### Pseudo force
- F_pseudo = −m a_frame

### Tricks
- Draw free body diagram first.
- For connected bodies, write equations separately for each body.
- Use friction direction opposite to relative tendency of motion.

## 5. Work, Energy and Power

### Work
- W = F s cos θ
- Work by gravity: W = mgh
- Work done by spring: W = 1/2 k(x₁² − x₂²)
- Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔK

### Energy
- Kinetic energy: K = 1/2 mv²
- Potential energy: U = mgh
- Spring energy: U = 1/2 kx²
- Mechanical energy = K + U

### Power
- Average power: P = W / t
- Instantaneous power: P = F·v

### Key concept
- Conservative force: path independent, mechanical energy conserved
- Non-conservative force: friction, air drag, heat generation

### Common errors
- Forgetting angle between force and displacement
- Not considering friction in energy conservation
- Wrong sign with work against gravity

## 6. Circular Motion

### Formulas
- Angular displacement: θ = s/r
- Angular velocity: ω = dθ/dt
- Angular acceleration: α = dω/dt
- Linear speed: v = rω
- Tangential acceleration: a_t = rα
- Radial acceleration: a_r = v²/r = rω²
- Centripetal force: F_c = mv²/r = mrω²
- Time period: T = 2π/ω

### Vertical circular motion
- At highest point, weight and tension act downward
- At lowest point, tension acts upward
- Use energy conservation between points

### Banking of roads
- tan θ = v²/(rg)

### Important note
- Centripetal force is not a new force; it is the net inward force required.

## 7. Centre of Mass and System of Particles

### COM formulas
- x_cm = Σ m_i x_i / Σ m_i
- y_cm = Σ m_i y_i / Σ m_i
- z_cm = Σ m_i z_i / Σ m_i

### Momentum
- P = M V_cm
- Total momentum of isolated system remains conserved

### Collision basics
- Elastic collision: kinetic energy conserved
- Inelastic collision: kinetic energy not conserved
- Coefficient of restitution: e = relative speed of separation / relative speed of approach

### Typical two-body collision relations
- v₁' = ((m₁ − m₂)/(m₁ + m₂)) v₁ + ((2m₂)/(m₁ + m₂)) v₂
- v₂' = ((2m₁)/(m₁ + m₂)) v₁ + ((m₂ − m₁)/(m₁ + m₂)) v₂

### Important concept
- In absence of external force, center of mass moves uniformly.

## 8. Rotational Motion

### Angular variables
- θ = s/r
- ω = dθ/dt
- α = dω/dt
- v = rω
- a_t = rα
- a_r = v²/r

### Equations of motion
- ω = ω₀ + αt
- θ = ω₀t + 1/2 αt²
- ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

### Torque and inertia
- τ = r × F
- τ = Iα
- I = Σ m_i r_i²
- Parallel axis theorem: I = I_cm + Md²
- Perpendicular axis theorem: I_z = I_x + I_y

### Angular momentum
- L = Iω
- τ = dL/dt
- Conservation of angular momentum

### Energy
- Rotational KE = 1/2 Iω²
- Total KE for rolling = translational + rotational

### Moment of inertia of common bodies
- Ring: MR²
- Disc: 1/2 MR²
- Solid sphere: 2/5 MR²
- Hollow sphere: 2/3 MR²
- Rod about center: 1/12 ML²
- Rod about end: 1/3 ML²

### Rolling without slipping
- v_cm = Rω

## 9. Gravitation

### Force and field
- F = Gm₁m₂/r²
- Gravitational field: g = GM/r²
- Potential energy: U = −GMm/r
- Gravitational potential: V = −GM/r

### Orbits
- Orbital speed: v = √(GM/r)
- Orbital time period: T = 2π√(r³/GM)
- Escape velocity: v_e = √(2GM/R)

### Variation of g
- At height h: g_h = g (R/(R+h))²
- At depth d: g_d = g(1 − d/R)

### Important note
- Weight decreases with height and depth.

## 10. Properties of Matter

### Stress and strain
- Stress = F / A
- Strain = ΔL / L
- Young's modulus: Y = stress / strain
- Bulk modulus: K = −ΔP / (ΔV/V)
- Shear modulus: η = shear stress / shear strain

### Elastic energy
- U = 1/2 × stress × strain × volume

### Fluids
- Pressure at depth: P = P₀ + ρgh
- Buoyant force: F_b = ρ V g
- Surface tension force = T × length

---

## 11. Fluid Mechanics

### Pressure and density
- Pressure: P = F/A
- Hydrostatic pressure: P = P₀ + ρgh
- Density: ρ = m/V

### Buoyancy
- Archimedes principle: buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid
- F_b = ρ_fluid V_displaced g

### Continuity equation
- A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

### Bernoulli equation
- P + ρgh + 1/2 ρv² = constant

### Viscosity
- Stokes law: F = 6π η r v
- Terminal velocity: v_t = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η

### Surface tension
- Excess pressure in spherical drop: ΔP = 2T/r
- Excess pressure in bubble: ΔP = 4T/r
- Capillary rise: h = 2T cosθ / (ρgr)

## 12. Thermodynamics

### Heat and expansion
- Q = mcΔT
- Q = mL
- Linear expansion: ΔL = αLΔT
- Area expansion: ΔA = βAΔT
- Volume expansion: ΔV = γVΔT
- β = 2α, γ = 3α

### Gas laws
- Boyle's law: PV = constant
- Charles' law: V/T = constant
- Gay-Lussac law: P/T = constant
- Ideal gas equation: PV = nRT

### Work
- W = ∫ P dV
- Isothermal work: W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁)
- Isobaric work: W = P(V₂ − V₁)

### First law
- ΔQ = ΔU + W

### Specific heats
- C_p − C_v = R
- γ = C_p / C_v

### Carnot efficiency
- η = 1 − T_c / T_h

## 13. Kinetic Theory of Gases

### Basic formulas
- P = 1/3 ρ c²
- PV = nRT
- v_rms = √(3RT/M)
- v_avg = √(8RT/πM)
- v_mp = √(2RT/M)
- Mean free path: λ = 1 / (√2 π d² N/V)

### Average kinetic energy
- Average KE per molecule = 3/2 kT
- Internal energy U = f/2 nRT

### Degrees of freedom
- Monoatomic: f = 3
- Diatomic: f = 5 (room temp), 7 (high temp)

## 14. Oscillations and SHM

### SHM formulas
- x = A sin(ωt + φ)
- v = Aω cos(ωt + φ)
- a = −ω² x
- ω = 2π/T = 2πf
- T = 2π√(m/k)
- T = 2π√(l/g)
- Energy: E = 1/2 kA²

### Spring and pendulum
- Spring constant: ω = √(k/m)
- Simple pendulum: T = 2π√(l/g)

### Important concept
- In SHM, restoring force is proportional to displacement and opposite in direction.

## 15. Waves

### Wave relation
- v = fλ
- ω = 2πf
- k = 2π/λ

### Travelling wave equation
- y = A sin(kx − ωt)
- y = A cos(kx − ωt)

### Stationary waves
- Closed pipe: f_n = (2n−1) v / 4L
- Open pipe: f_n = n v / 2L
- Fixed-end string: f_n = n v / 2L

### Beats
- Beat frequency = |f₁ − f₂|

### Doppler effect
- f' = f (v ± v₀)/(v ∓ v_s)

## 16. Electrostatics

### Coulomb law
- F = k q₁q₂ / r²
- k = 1/(4πϵ₀)

### Electric field
- E = F/q
- Point charge: E = kQ/r²
- Force on charge: F = qE

### Potential and energy
- Potential: V = kQ/r
- Potential difference: ΔV = W/q
- Potential energy: U = k q₁q₂ / r

### Dipole
- Dipole moment: p = q × 2l
- Torque: τ = pE sin θ
- Potential energy: U = −pE cos θ
- Axial field: E = 2kp / r³
- Equatorial field: E = kp / r³

### Gauss law
- Φ = ∮ E·dA = Q_enclosed / ε₀
- Infinite line: E = λ/(2πϵ₀r)
- Infinite sheet: E = σ/(2ϵ₀)

### Capacitance
- C = Q/V
- Parallel plate: C = ε₀A/d
- Series: 1/C_eq = Σ 1/C_i
- Parallel: C_eq = Σ C_i
- Energy: U = 1/2 CV² = Q²/(2C)
- Dielectric: C = K C₀

## 17. Current Electricity

### Ohm's law
- V = IR
- Resistivity: ρ = RA/L
- Conductivity: σ = 1/ρ

### Current and drift velocity
- I = nqAv_d
- J = σE
- v_d = eEτ/m

### Resistance
- Series: R_eq = ΣR_i
- Parallel: 1/R_eq = Σ1/R_i
- Temperature dependence: R = R₀(1 + αΔT)

### Power
- P = VI = I²R = V²/R
- Electrical energy = VIt

### Cells
- E = V + Ir
- Terminal voltage: V = E − Ir

### Kirchhoff laws
- Junction law: ΣI = 0
- Loop law: ΣV = 0

## 18. Magnetism

### Force on moving charge
- F = q(v × B)
- F = qvB sin θ
- Radius: r = mv/(qB)
- Time period: T = 2πm/(qB)
- Frequency: f = qB/(2πm)

### Current in magnetic field
- F = I (l × B)
- F = B I l sin θ

### Magnetic field due to current
- Long straight wire: B = μ₀I/(2πr)
- Circular loop at center: B = μ₀I/(2R)
- Solenoid: B = μ₀nI
- Toroid: B = μ₀NI/(2πr)

### Magnetic dipole
- Magnetic moment: μ = NIA
- Torque: τ = μ × B
- Potential energy: U = −μ·B

---

## 19. Electromagnetic Induction

### Faraday and Lenz law
- Φ = B·A = BA cos θ
- ε = −dΦ/dt
- Motional emf: ε = B l v

### Self and mutual inductance
- ε = −L dI/dt
- ε₂ = −M dI₁/dt
- Energy in inductor: U = 1/2 LI²

### RL circuit
- Time constant: τ = L/R

### LC oscillation
- ω = 1/√(LC)

## 20. Alternating Current

### RMS values
- I_rms = I₀/√2
- V_rms = V₀/√2

### Reactance
- X_L = ωL
- X_C = 1/(ωC)
- Impedance: Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²)

### Power
- P_avg = V_rms I_rms cos φ
- Power factor: cos φ = R/Z

### Resonance
- X_L = X_C
- ω₀ = 1/√(LC)

## 21. Electromagnetic Waves

### Maxwell relations
- c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)
- E = cB
- Speed in medium: v = 1/√(μϵ)

### Energy density
- u_E = 1/2 ε₀E²
- u_B = B²/(2μ₀)
- Poynting vector: S = (1/μ₀) E × B

## 22. Ray Optics

### Reflection
- Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
- Magnification: m = −v/u
- For spherical mirror: f = R/2

### Refraction
- Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
- Refractive index: n = c/v
- Apparent depth: d_app = d / n

### Lens formula
- 1/f = 1/v − 1/u
- Lens maker's formula: 1/f = (n_rel − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)
- Magnification: m = v/u

### Prism
- δ = (n − 1)A (small angle approximation)

### Optical instruments
- Simple microscope: m = 1 + D/f
- Telescope: m = f₀/fₑ

## 23. Wave Optics

### Interference
- Constructive: Δ = mλ
- Destructive: Δ = (m + 1/2) λ
- Fringe width: β = λD/d

### Diffraction
- Single slit minima: a sin θ = nλ

### Polarization
- Malus law: I = I₀ cos²θ
- Brewster angle: tan i_B = n₂/n₁

## 24. Modern Physics

### Photoelectric effect
- E = hν = hc/λ
- K_max = hν − φ
- φ = hν₀

### De Broglie wavelength
- λ = h / p = h/(mv)

### Bohr model
- mvr = nh/2π
- E_n = −13.6 Z² / n² eV
- Radius: r_n = 0.529 n²/Z Å

### Radioactivity
- N = N₀ e^(−λt)
- Half-life: T1/2 = ln2 / λ
- Average life: τ = 1/λ

### Binding energy
- E_b = Δm c²

### Bragg law
- 2d sin θ = nλ
